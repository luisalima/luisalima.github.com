[While writing yesterday's note](/notes/ask-before-you-install/), I realized that some malware sat on npm for six months before the advisory database most tools read caught up. So, I wondered... how fast does the news about a *vulnerability* travel? I picked 20 CVEs at random and split them up into two groups of 10: the "loud" ones (exploited in the wild: attackers are already using them) and the "quiet" ones (ordinary bugs in npm and PyPI packages).

1. A CVE is an entry in a public catalog of known security bugs. Most of us start paying attention to a bug the day its CVE is published.
2. But let's take **Acronis** (CVE-2026-87886) as an example. The first post about it on X {x} came out... **45 hours before** its CVE record! <!-- chart: Acronis, X only -->
3. News {news} followed, 16 hours **before** the CVE record. And CISA {kev} (the US government's cybersecurity agency) had already listed it as **exploited in the wild**, the day before the record existed. <!-- chart: Acronis, X + news + KEV -->
4. Only then did the CVE record land. <!-- chart: the record line pulses -->
5. Is this unusual? No. Of 10 bugs exploited in the wild, **5 were public somewhere before their CVE record**: on X, Reddit, in the news, on GitHub or on CISA's list. <!-- chart: all 10 exploited, "before" glows -->
6. Public exploit code {poc} came later, **15 hours to 22 days** after the first mention on socials and news. But attackers didn't wait for public exploit code: for 4 of those 6, CISA {kev} had already listed the bug as exploited in the wild. <!-- chart: exploit routes -->
7. Then I looked at 10 ordinary bugs in npm and PyPI packages. Before their CVE record, there was **nothing**. After it, there was a handful of posts and some aggregator news. <!-- chart: the 10 ordinary bugs -->
8. But... look left. Every one of them was **fixed before its CVE** {fix}: 25 days earlier for the typical bug, up to 73. So, the fix was public weeks before the record appeared. This is what we like ;) <!-- chart: fix diamonds slide back -->
9. So the CVE record appeared **after the attackers** for the loud bugs, and **weeks after the fix** for the quiet ones. <!-- chart: same; ✍️ your line -->
10. What does this mean for you? For many bugs, the fix is out **weeks before** the CVE record that your tools are waiting for. So, how can we take fixes when they ship and not wait? <!-- chart: the update flow appears -->
11. Being fast doesn't mean being careless (remember yesterday's note?). The update bot can wait a few days, CI can block install scripts, the scanners can check the lists (can be late, but always check) and the tests decide whether to merge. Even with a 7-day wait before updating, all 10 fixes would have landed before their CVE record. <!-- chart: steps 2-4 lit -->
12. Sometimes tests can't tell you if an update will break your app in unexpected ways, and the people who could check are overwhelmed. Use the agents! **Agents can click through the change and record a walkthrough video**, so a person approves in minutes instead of reading the whole diff. For backend, they can use curl and other tools and do the same thing. <!-- chart: walkthrough step lit -->
13. **So, have the systems in place so you can update fast, and remember that no single measure keeps your code and apps safe: it's defense in depth.** <!-- chart: the whole flow; your line, Luisa -->
